# Supplement protocols — reviewed 22 October 2026

These are selected personal schedules, not personalized prescriptions. Research is mainly in adults; adolescent use needs clinician review. Intake logging does not measure tolerance or neurotransmitters. Choose third-party-tested products and review overlapping ingredients.

| Supplement | Dose | Frequency | Timing | Purpose & evidence | Why this schedule? | Important notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium Multivitamin | 2 tablets; verify the product serving size. | Daily. | With breakfast or another meal containing some fat. | Helps fill dietary micronutrient gaps. B vitamins support normal energy metabolism; they do not provide extra energy when intake is already adequate. | No routine cycling needed at appropriate label doses. Product ingredients are not confirmed from the name alone. | Check total B6, vitamin A, zinc and iron across products. Excess is not all harmlessly excreted; chronic excess B6 can damage nerves. Methylated forms are not universally superior. Prefer a third-party-tested product. |
| Vitamin K2 (MK-7) | 100 mcg. | Daily. | With a meal containing fat; can accompany the multivitamin. | Supports vitamin-K-dependent proteins involved in clotting and bone metabolism. | No established cycling requirement. It is not proven to remove arterial calcium or prevent cardiovascular disease in everyone. | Major interaction with warfarin: do not start, stop or change intake without the prescriber. Check whether the multivitamin already contains vitamin K. |
| Omega-3 (rTG Fish Oil) | 1 softgel labeled 1,250 mg fish oil; verify EPA + DHA separately. | Daily. | With food, at a convenient meal. | EPA and DHA contribute to cell membranes and signaling; DHA is important in neural tissue. Benefits depend on diet and the outcome being treated. | No routine cycling requirement. Total oil weight does not equal active EPA + DHA content. | May cause fishy burps or stomach upset. Review anticoagulant use. High-dose 4 g/day regimens increased atrial fibrillation in some cardiovascular-risk trials; do not escalate casually. There is no universal 3,000 mg bleeding threshold. |
| Magnesium Glycinate | Your listed range: 210–420 mg; confirm this is elemental magnesium and verify capsule count. | Daily; review the higher dose. | Evening, 30–60 minutes before bed if convenient; food may improve tolerance. | Supports normal nerve, muscle and enzyme function. Evidence for improving sleep in people without deficiency is limited. | No cycling requirement. There is no universal need to avoid ordinary calcium-containing meals. | 420 mg exceeds the US 350 mg/day upper limit from supplements/medicines unless clinician-directed; food magnesium is excluded. Diarrhea is common with excess; kidney impairment raises toxicity risk. Separate from certain antibiotics and osteoporosis medicines according to pharmacist instructions. |
| Creatine Monohydrate | 10 g/day — your selected target; usual maintenance is 3–5 g/day. | Daily, including rest days. | Any consistent time. If using 10 g, split into 5 g twice daily to improve stomach tolerance. | Increases muscle phosphocreatine availability for repeated high-intensity efforts and training adaptation. | No routine cycling needed. More than the usual maintenance dose does not automatically improve results. | Can cause water-weight gain and GI upset. Hydrate for heat, sweat and thirst; no universal 100+ oz requirement. Discuss use with a clinician if you have kidney disease; tell them before kidney blood tests, since creatinine can rise without proving kidney injury. |
| L-Citrulline (Pure Base) | 6–8 g — your selected dose. | Daily by your preference; daily use is not an established requirement. | Workout days: 45–60 minutes before training. Rest days: any consistent time. | Raises arginine availability for nitric-oxide production; exercise-performance findings are mixed. | No established cycling rule. Long-term daily safety at this dose is less well characterized than creatine. | May upset the stomach or lower blood pressure. Review use with blood-pressure medicines, nitrates or erectile-dysfunction medicines. Pure citrulline and citrulline malate are not gram-for-gram equivalent. Added salt is optional, not required. |
| CDP-Choline (Citicoline) | 300 mg. | 3 nonconsecutive days/week, e.g. Mon/Wed/Sat — your chosen schedule. | Morning or before cognitive demand; avoid late dosing if it disrupts sleep. | Supplies choline for acetylcholine and phospholipid synthesis. Some trials suggest memory benefits; faster processing and better learning are not guaranteed. | Spacing limits exposure and gives off-days to assess your response. It is not proven necessary to prevent acetylcholine toxicity: 500 mg daily has been studied for 12 weeks. A long metabolite half-life does not establish a toxic ceiling. | “Choline blues” is not a validated diagnosis or inevitable daily-use effect. Stop and reassess new brain fog, jaw tension, low mood or lethargy rather than assuming a neurotransmitter cause; seek care for persistent symptoms. Daily trial use does not establish indefinite safety for everyone. |
| L-Tyrosine (Free-form) | 500 mg — your selected dose; benefit at this dose is uncertain. | 1–3 times/week for demanding days — your personal cap, not a validated safety threshold. | Prefer between meals, away from a protein-heavy meal or shake; your planned timing is 30–45 minutes before demand. | Precursor for dopamine and norepinephrine. Small studies suggest benefits under acute stress or demanding tasks, not a guaranteed everyday dopamine boost. | Other large neutral amino acids compete for transport into the brain. Separating a dose from protein may make exposure more predictable; taking it with food does not make it toxic or automatically ineffective. No precise fasting interval is established. | No good human evidence shows ordinary daily use inevitably downregulates tyrosine hydroxylase or damages baseline dopamine production. Limit unnecessary use because long-term benefit/safety is uncertain, not to prevent a proven shutdown. Do not increase the dose just to chase an effect; review medicines and health conditions with a pharmacist. |
| KSM-66 Ashwagandha | 600 mg/day during ON periods; extract strengths vary. | Your cycle: 4–8 weeks ON, 2–4 weeks OFF; reassess before restarting. | Evening; with food if needed for stomach tolerance. No established need to take it immediately post-workout. | Some preparations may reduce stress and improve sleep; results are extract-specific and studies are generally short. | Evidence mainly covers short-term use, up to about 3 months. This cycle is not proven to prevent tolerance, anhedonia, receptor changes or liver injury. | Can cause drowsiness/GI upset; rare liver injury and thyroid effects matter. Stop and seek care for jaundice, dark urine or severe abdominal pain. Avoid during pregnancy/breastfeeding and review thyroid/autoimmune disease, surgery, sedatives and blood-pressure/diabetes medicines. Persistent emotional blunting warrants stopping and assessment. |
| L-Theanine | 200 mg. | Daily or as needed; skipping is fine. | Evening for relaxation/sleep, or with daytime caffeine. Your preference: skip immediately before heavy lifting unless deliberately pairing with caffeine. | May support relaxation and some sleep measures. Alpha-wave effects are not proof of better sleep or cognition for everyone. | A small trial studied 200 mg daily for 4 weeks. No established cycling requirement; long-term data are limited. | Some people feel too relaxed or drowsy for heavy training; avoid pre-workout use if this occurs. It is not proven to reduce strength or “athletic aggression” universally. Caffeine does not guarantee reversal of drowsiness, and theanine does not make excessive stimulant doses safe. Watch additive drowsiness with other calming products. |
